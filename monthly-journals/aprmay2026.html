I JUST GRADUADO

Anyway, April was an NPC kind of month, so I’ll recap quickly. I think there are two things worth mentioning:

  1. Vacation to La Push + the coastline along the Pacific: we stayed at this really cute hotel on the beach and saw many sunsets/whales/sea birds/stars. The area we were staying in had no wifi/data, EXCEPT for this one random spot in the middle of a bunch of trees. This was very funny because it meant that everyone staying at the hotel (including me) would drive to this spot and huddle in their cars while they downloaded their favorite shows at 11 PM. I definitely got rage-baited after getting blinded by 5 million (5) cars with their high beams on. My TV show of choice was the Queen’s Gambit, which I highly recommend!!
  2. Senioritis: I’m actually surprised it took until the last week of April for senioritis to actually kick in. With senioritis, “completion-based” meant “optional” and “due Monday” meant “due Tuesday evening”. Yes, this means I was clicking C for all the “completion-based” multiple choice assignments and submitting them in under 30 seconds. (But how necessary were those practice assignments if I could get 100s on the tests anyway???)

Speaking of May flowers, there’s a surplus of flowers and bouquets on the dinner table from various events like prom, choir concerts, and graduation, which were all events that occurred during

Grad week

In one word: overwhelming.

I guess I had heard all throughout high school that grad week was SUPER FUN, which is why I had high expectations. If you’re not familiar with how grad week at my school works, it goes something like

Tuesday – last day of school + senior sunset

Wednesday – class day (AKA we all make posters about college + moving away and cry)

Thursday – Baccalaureate Mass

Friday – Prom

Saturday – NOTHING! + various peoples’ grad parties

Sunday – Graduation

Monday – grad night

Theoretically, all these events are super fun and amazing, and I’m grateful I got to experience them. At the same time, having these big events in one week meant my emotional (and physical) state was fragile at best. Wearing high heels is not for the weak… Also, the 30-min rehearsals held 3 hours before the actual event lowkenuinely pmo’ed because why did we practice walking in a line (iykyk).

I’d like to thank my sister for getting me through this week, because I definitely crashed out more than I should’ve.

Lessons learned from grad week

  1. Don’t stress over things you can’t control
  2. Don’t wear high heels for more than 2 hours at a time
  3. Breathe or escape when overwhelmed
  4. Alter your dress prior to the day before prom
  5. Live in the moment instead of trying to get the perfect picture
  6. Smile with your teeth
