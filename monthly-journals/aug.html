Dear friends and family,

Although summer isn’t technically over until September 22, it’s felt over for a few weeks at this point. It’s still sunny and warm, but the sun setting earlier and earlier each day reminds me of the upcoming fall.

Starting the school year

Senior year seemed to start slow at first, before becoming pretty intense very quickly. Still, some things are different. There are new faces in the school building, reminding me of the old faces I don’t get to see daily anymore. It’s a bittersweet feeling of FOMO, knowing that some of your friends have moved on from their high school era without you. I can’t quite explain how it feels to be the next graduating class. I’m nervous and excited, but mostly I get the sense that I’ll blink and suddenly be graduating in a white gown and cap.

Other things that are different:

  • Instead of passing out over the thin pages of a multi-pound textbook, I now lapse into spontaneous naps over my laptop keyboard. My back is grateful for the convenience of electronic textbooks, and I’m no longer hauling what feels like a bag of rocks up four flights of stairs daily. But still, I sometimes miss the soft paper that sticks to my cheek after I lift my head from my textbook-turned-pillow.
  • Senior privileges. In a way, I feel spoiled to be the first to leave all-school assemblies and have off-campus lunch. Of course, this comes with a healthy amount of gloating towards the underclassmen, but I’m sure they’ll feel the same once they’re in my position. Our special senior year locker is already filled with candy, snacks, and laugh-worthy decorations (props to my locker-mates).
  • Driving with friends. I started driving a little later than most people, and I only began driving myself to school in the spring of junior year. The convenience of knowing how to drive has been life-changing: I appreciate having one less thing to worry about besides the normal stressors of senior year. But using the car also means stealing it from my mom… sorry!!

Free Will

I sometimes forget that I have free will, which sounds like the stupidest thing ever, but it’s true. I’m the sort of person who likes to plan things out super thoroughly, but the downside to that is limiting myself from exploring. I get so caught up in following my self-imposed instructions (that don’t always keep my happiness and health in mind, believe it or not) that I often become restricted from doing the things that actually bring me joy. Some people tell me that college is the place for exploration, not high school, but then I remember I have free will and can explore whenever/wherever/however I want.

Remembering that I have free will has been useful in several situations within the past month. On one particularly busy Tuesday, I found myself finishing up homework at 1 AM since I had not arrived home until late in the evening. I completed my second to last assignment due the next day, when I suddenly realized that I had free will. What was stopping me from achieving my well-earned and much-needed sleep? Did it really matter if I did my non-graded, non-collected homework a day late? The answer was no, it didn’t matter, and I found myself prioritizing my health and happiness for the 3rd time in my high school career (half-joking).

My goal for senior year: know when to do homework a day late exercise my free will.

College applications

So far, I have pretty much failed to assert my dominance over the college application process. How it’s going as of now:

  • Was a little too chalant while having my 2nd (or 3rd, I’ve lost track) 1/5th life crisis of the year. Seriously, what do I major in???
  • Stared at a blank Google Docs for far too long (the blinking cursor is the bane of my existence).
  • Took one glance at a certain supplemental question and sat in bed for an hour, questioning existence.
  • Wrote 5 different essays for the same prompt and couldn’t choose between them.
